## Summary

| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Can these inputs predict the **direction** of a 5-day move? | **No.** Best pooled ROC-AUC 0.512 (0.5 = coin flip); every ML model has negative Brier skill. | Direction tables below |
| Do the product's old **hand-written indicator rules** carry signal? | Essentially none. RSI mean reversion has rank IC 0.020; "above EMA50 = bullish" is slightly *negative*. | `rule_*` rows |
| Does any strategy built on these scores beat buy-and-hold after costs? | **No.** Net Sharpe -0.15 to 0.13 vs 0.80 for the equal-weight benchmark. | Strategy columns |
| Can the **size** of the next 5 days' moves be predicted? | **Yes.** Ridge beats repeating recent volatility by about 12% MSE, in 14 of 14 folds; 80% intervals cover 79%. | Volatility tables |
| Does that hold on unseen data? | Yes: holdout R² 0.243, 9.9% lower MSE than persistence, 79.6% coverage. The v1.1.0 holdout is a *second* look (see below). | Holdout table |

## Why the holdout is not pristine for v1.1.0

v1.0.0 was scored once on the holdout. A test then showed its forecast *fell* when a stock's own volatility rose in
18 of 25 sampled cases, because raw and log volatility features were collinear. v1.1.0 fixes the specification and
adds a monotonicity requirement. Its holdout score is a second look at the same period, so it is reported as such.
The two versions scored almost identically (R² 0.2426 vs 0.2434), which suggests the fix did not overfit the holdout.

## What these numbers do not show

- Performance on stocks outside the 47 current Nifty 50 constituents (survivorship bias: stocks that left the index are absent).
- Intraday behaviour, or any horizon other than 5 trading days.
- Any benefit from news, fundamentals or macro data: none of these are model inputs (no point-in-time history).
- Statistical significance of differences between the ML direction models: they are all indistinguishable from chance.
